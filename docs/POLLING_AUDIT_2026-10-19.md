# Polling audit: which metrics need fast polling (AWS, Azure, GCP)

Generated by `scripts/polling_audit.py` from the real catalogs and tier maps (404 scheduled metrics; full list in
`docs/polling_inventory.csv`). Re-run it any time; `--check` is also a unit test.

## 1. What decides how often a metric is polled

**The metric, not the alert priority.** Priority (P1-P4) says who is told first. It never changes how often data is
collected. Polling interval comes from the metric's tier:

| Tier | AWS | Azure | GCP | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| critical | 2 min | 2 min | 2 min | availability, error and saturation signals; alerts also evaluated every 2 min |
| standard | 5 min | 5 min | 5 min | alertable signals, or a source that itself publishes every 5 min |
| low | 15 min | 15 min | 15 min | trend and capacity |
| extended | 60 min | **15 min** | 60 min | informational metrics of extended services |
| slow_extended | 24 h | 60 min | none | daily publishers (S3 storage, certificate expiry) |

The only thing that can make polling depend on environment is `ENV_POLLING_ENABLED` (Phase 4c): UAT non-critical
x3, DEV non-critical hourly. It is **off**. `docs/POLLING_COST_ESTIMATE.md` section 6 measured the saving at about
$3.60 a month on PROD (15.5% of billed metrics) and decided not to enable it.

## 2. Findings

Method: for every metric, compare its interval with (a) how fast the provider publishes it and (b) whether an
incident depends on it. Every proposal below was checked against the catalog (the script fails if a metric does
not exist or the change would not move it in the intended direction).

### 2a. Polled too slowly (fixed in this change, 25 metrics)

A failure or saturation signal on the hourly or daily tier is noticed only after the poll plus the evaluation
window (up to about 75 min hourly, a day for daily), while sibling services were polled every 5-15 min.

| Cloud | Service: metrics | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| AWS | CloudFront: 5xxErrorRate, TotalErrorRate | 24 h | 15 min |
| AWS | API Gateway: Latency, IntegrationLatency | 60 min | 15 min |
| AWS | DocumentDB: CPUUtilization, FreeableMemory; Neptune, Redshift, OpenSearch, MemoryDB: CPUUtilization; OpenSearch: FreeStorageSpace | 60 min | 15 min |
| AWS | ElastiCache: ReplicationLag; DMS: CDCLatencySource, CDCLatencyTarget; Transit Gateway: PacketDropCountBlackhole, PacketDropCountNoRoute | 60 min | 15 min |
| Azure | AKS: kube_node_status_condition (node NotReady) | 15 min | 5 min |
| GCP | GKE node: cpu and memory allocatable utilization, ephemeral storage used | 60 min | 15 min |
| GCP | Spanner: cpu utilization, api request latencies; Redis: cpu utilization; NAT: dropped packets, port usage | 60 min | 15 min |

Why 15 and not 2: none of these is an outage on its own, and the 2-minute tier is kept for availability and error
signals. Nothing here is made faster than 5 min.

Cost: each promoted AWS metric adds 72 billed metrics a day per resource (CloudFront 95), about $0.02 a month per
resource at the list price. The Overview shows EC2, EBS, ALB, S3, ACM, CloudWatch Logs and EventBridge for the two accounts, with
15 more services hidden behind "+more", so check the per-service counts on PROD before assuming the cost is zero. Azure and GCP: no per-call charge modelled.

### 2b. Polled faster than any alert needs (NOT applied)

Traffic-volume meters on the 5-minute tier (EC2 NetworkIn/Out, ALB and NLB connection and flow counts, ProcessedBytes,
Azure VM network and storage Transactions, GCP network bytes and request/instance counts), and one on the 2-minute tier:
**ALB RequestCount**. These carry the placeholder threshold (`threshold_defaults.py`, "PLACEHOLDER thresholds"), so they
alert only when far outside the resource's own baseline and never above WARNING. A metric that can never be CRITICAL
gains nothing from the 2-minute critical tick, and 15 minutes would do for the rest.

At a fleet of 40 EC2 and 3 load balancers the AWS saving is at most about $6 a month (it counts every load balancer for every connection and flow meter), and a network or traffic anomaly would be
noticed up to 10 minutes later. That is not worth changing alerting behaviour today. The list is `DEMOTIONS` in the script,
with a cost model (`--counts '{"ec2": 40, "elb": 3}'`). Changing RequestCount means moving it out of the P1 set, which the
priority and polling tests treat as a critical-class key, so it needs its own patch.

### 2c. Looked at and left alone

- **WAF** is already on its own 1-minute collector.
- **EC2 status checks and ALB healthy hosts** use the free Describe API every 60 s.
- **Daily publishers** (S3 storage, ACM and KMS expiry) are correctly daily.
- **RDS CPU, connections, FreeableMemory and ALB 5xx** are real outage signals and stay at 2 minutes.

## 3. Solution: three layers, in this order

1. **Metric class (done here).** Fixes the interval to what the metric is for. Biggest effect on detection time, nearly no cost.
2. **Environment (built, off).** `ENV_POLLING_ENABLED` slows non-critical metrics on UAT and DEV. Keep it off until the
   fleet or its non-prod share grows (the break-even is in `POLLING_COST_ESTIMATE.md` section 5).
3. **Resource criticality (proposal, not built).** `Criticality=low` resources would get the UAT treatment even in
   PROD, `Criticality=critical` ones would never be slowed. It needs the Criticality tag filled in first:
   209 of 334 resources are still unclassified, so there is nothing reliable to key on yet.

Until the tags exist, further slowing would be guesswork, and at about $25 a month of GetMetricData the cost is not
the problem; detection time on the metrics in 2a was.
